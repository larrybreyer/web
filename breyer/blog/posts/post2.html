1984 — Newspeak Mini‑Dictionary

A concise glossary of major Newspeak words and Party terms with plain English definitions and book examples.

This page lists key Newspeak terms from George Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four, with simple definitions and short examples drawn from the novel. Use the footnotes for source links and background reading.

Term Definition & example
Bellyfeel Accepting an idea instinctively and emotionally, without needing logic or evidence. Example: A Party member might bellyfeel that “War is Peace” simply because it feels right.
Big Brother The leader figure and symbol of the Party’s control; always watching citizens. Example: Posters declaring BIG BROTHER IS WATCHING YOU remind people they are under surveillance.
Blackwhite Believing something because the Party says it, even if it conflicts with memory or fact; forgetting you once believed otherwise. Example: Allies become enemies overnight; blackwhite makes you accept the reversal without question.
Crimestop The mental skill of stopping yourself from forming rebellious or unorthodox thoughts. Example: When a dangerous doubt begins, crimestop prevents it from developing into thoughtcrime.
Doublethink Holding two contradictory ideas at once and believing both. Example: Knowing the Ministry of Truth lies, while also accepting its official version of events.
DuckSpeak Speaking without thinking—parroting Party slogans or propaganda. Example: Repeating praise for a ration increase even though the ration was cut.
Facecrime A facial expression or mannerism that reveals unapproved thoughts. Example: A shocked face during a Party announcement could be treated as a facecrime.
Goodthink Thoughts that fully agree with Party ideology. Example: Unquestioning support of the Party’s policies is goodthink.
Ignorance is Strength A Party slogan asserting that not knowing facts makes society stronger and more stable. Example: The Party keeps historical records secret to maintain its power.
Ingsoc Short for "English Socialism," the ruling ideology of Oceania. Example: Party members are taught Ingsoc’s principles at school and work.
Inner Party The small, powerful elite who control Oceania and the Party’s decisions. Example: Inner Party members like O’Brien decide what is truthful and enforce it.
Miniluv Ministry of Love; enforces loyalty through fear, arrest, and torture. Example: Suspected thoughtcriminals are taken to Miniluv for interrogation.
Minipax Ministry of Peace; manages the state of permanent war. Example: Minipax announces victories that help unify the population.
Miniplenty Ministry of Plenty; in charge of rations, production claims, and economic reports—often the cause of shortages. Example: Miniplenty may report a rise in goods while citizens face empty shelves.
Minitrue Ministry of Truth; rewrites history, controls media, and spreads propaganda. Example: Minitrue alters newspapers to match the Party's present claims.
Newspeak The simplified, controlled language designed to make rebellious thought impossible. Example: Removing or simplifying words that express dissent to limit the ability to think rebellion.
Oldspeak Standard English used before Newspeak existed. Example: Books written in Oldspeak are often destroyed to erase prior ideas.
Ownlife Individualism or living for oneself rather than serving the Party—considered antisocial. Example: Private hobbies or going alone may be judged as ownlife.
Outer Party The larger group of Party members who perform work and are closely monitored by the state. Example: Outer Party members have fewer privileges and more surveillance than Inner Party members.
Proles The working-class majority, mostly left unmonitored because the Party views them as politically harmless. Example: Winston thinks the proles might be the only hope for rebellion—but they are largely unconcerned with politics.
Thoughtcrime Thinking anything that opposes the Party’s ideology; it is treated as a serious offense. Example: Doubting Big Brother in private is considered thoughtcrime.
Unperson Someone erased from records and memory, as if they never existed. Example: After an arrest and disappearance, the Party removes all traces so the person becomes an unperson.
War is Peace A Party slogan that claims constant war keeps a society united and under control. Example: Endless conflict distracts citizens from domestic issues that might cause unrest.
